# Quality assurance checklist

## Visual identity

- [ ] A specific target year or narrow period is apparent.
- [ ] The selected website lineage fits the business.
- [ ] Typography, colors, layout, borders, images, and details belong to one system.
- [ ] Graphics are relevant to the business and not generic retro filler.
- [ ] No accidental mix of early-web, GeoCities, late-90s, Y2K, and modern SaaS tropes.
- [ ] The result looks intentionally art-directed, not like a theme pasted over the old site.

## Content and conversion

- [ ] The business and offer are still immediately understandable.
- [ ] Main CTA remains visible and works.
- [ ] Important pages and content sections remain present.
- [ ] Pricing, metrics, testimonials, customer names, and claims remain accurate.
- [ ] Contact details and external links remain correct.

## Functionality

- [ ] Internal navigation and routes work.
- [ ] Forms submit correctly and retain validation/error states.
- [ ] Menus, tabs, accordions, filters, search, and modals work.
- [ ] Authentication, checkout, integrations, and dynamic data were not broken.
- [ ] No dead links or placeholder controls.

## Responsive and accessible behavior

- [ ] Desktop composition is visually coherent.
- [ ] Tablet layout remains usable.
- [ ] Mobile navigation and content reflow work.
- [ ] No unintended horizontal overflow.
- [ ] Keyboard navigation and visible focus work.
- [ ] Contrast and text readability are adequate.
- [ ] Images have appropriate alt behavior.
- [ ] Reduced-motion preference is respected.

## Technical quality

- [ ] Lint passed or known issues are disclosed.
- [ ] Typecheck passed or known issues are disclosed.
- [ ] Tests passed or known issues are disclosed.
- [ ] Production build passed or known issues are disclosed.
- [ ] Browser console and network errors checked where possible.
- [ ] SEO metadata and analytics preserved.
- [ ] No unrelated user changes were overwritten.

## Required screenshots

When browser tooling is available, capture:

1. Homepage desktop, first viewport
2. Homepage desktop, full page
3. Homepage mobile
4. At least one representative interior page desktop
5. At least one representative interior page mobile
6. Important interactive states (menu, form, or primary conversion flow)

## Final review questions

- Would a designer familiar with the selected period recognize the references?
- Would a customer still trust and understand this business?
- Is the experience consistent across the site?
- Did we preserve what was working?
- What remains unverified?
