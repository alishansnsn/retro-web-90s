# Historical and technical research sources

Use these as starting points for research. Prefer primary screenshots and archived pages when available. Verify the date of each example; do not assume a site's current design represents its 1990s appearance.

## Historical galleries and timelines

- Web Design Museum — Web Design in the 90s: https://www.webdesignmuseum.org/exhibitions/web-design-in-the-90s
- Web Design Museum — 1990s archive/gallery: https://www.webdesignmuseum.org/web-design-in-the-90s
- Web Design Museum — Web Design History timeline: https://www.webdesignmuseum.org/web-design-history
- Internet Archive Wayback Machine: https://web.archive.org/

## Web history and technical context

- CERN — The birth of the Web: https://home.cern/science/computing/birth-web
- W3C — A Little History of the World Wide Web: https://www.w3.org/History.html
- W3C — CSS history and specifications: https://www.w3.org/Style/CSS/

## Research method

1. Search for a site from the selected year and business category.
2. Open its archived version in the Wayback Machine or a trustworthy historical gallery.
3. Inspect screenshots at the target viewport if available.
4. Record concrete evidence: page dimensions, column arrangement, navigation, colors, typography, graphic types, and content density.
5. Compare multiple examples. A single site's quirks should not be mistaken for a universal convention.
6. Cite or record sources in the design notes when the research materially informs the direction.

The references in this file are research entry points, not proof that every listed design convention existed in every year or browser.
